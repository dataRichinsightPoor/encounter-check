01 / The well

A flat-bottom 96-well has about 32 mm² of growth area and a flat 384-well about 5.6–6 mm² (Corning). A U- or V-bottom concentrates cells into a smaller, undeclared contact area; enter an effective value if you have one.

02 / Contact and killing

k × effector density is the encounter rate each free target experiences. It must be calibrated for the geometry; it is not a diffusion constant.

03 / The decision

Area change 0.175 approximates moving from a 96-well to a 384-well flat bottom. The contact rival multiplies k and re-solves the kill probability so both explanations match the reference lysis.

04 / Bispecific bridge

With the bridge on, the kill probability in 02 becomes the maximum. Each conjugate kills with probability p·(1 − e−sB), where B is the bridge index at the free drug concentration. Contact frequency is unchanged by the drug.

WHEN IS THE RATIO ENOUGH?

Search time decides which variable matters.

Each effector alternates between searching and handling a target. When searching dominates, the killing rate per target scales with effector density and the E:T ratio is not a sufficient description. When handling dominates, effectors are saturated with targets and the fraction killed follows the ratio and the clock. The search fraction below is computed at time zero from the declared rates.

0 · handling-limited, ratio governs1 · search-limited, density governs

Search-limited

Effectors spend most of their time searching. Per-target killing follows effector density, so at a fixed ratio a denser well kills faster.

Mixed

Search and handling take comparable time. Density and ratio both move the endpoint, and neither alone describes the condition.

Handling-limited

Effectors are saturated with targets. Each kills at close to its maximum rate, so the fraction killed tracks the ratio and the clock.

Single-use ceiling

Effectors do not return after killing. Failed contacts can be retried, so lysis creeps toward the ratio itself and can never exceed it, however long the assay runs.

A PRACTICAL DECISION

Fewer targets. Which well reproduces the reference?

When fewer target cells are available, keeping the ratio in the same well also lowers both densities. In this model only preserving both densities preserves the experiment, which means changing the well, not the ratio. Crowding and settled-area assumptions still have to hold in the smaller well.

DISCRIMINATING EXPERIMENT 3

Same dose, different free drug.

A bispecific links two cells only when one arm holds a target antigen and the other finds a free effector receptor. Density now enters twice. More cells per area mean more contacts, which raises the plateau. More cells per volume bind more drug, which leaves less free drug at the same nominal concentration and moves the curve right. The two effects can offset each other at a working dose, so a flat response across density does not show that the ratio suffices.

The bispecific bridge is off. Switch it on in 04, or load the example “Same ratio, same dose.” Everything above then uses the drug-dependent kill probability.

WHAT THIS MODEL IS NOT

Boundaries, stated plainly.

Scope. A well-mixed, short-window, mass-action model of one effector population meeting one target population, with one contact state, one kill transition, optional effector recovery, and first-order background death. It excludes proliferation, activation delay, exhaustion, cytokine effects, multicellular conjugates, cooperative killing, target heterogeneity, motility-limited or spatially aggregated contact, and adherent geometry. Each could change the ranking.
The bridge. Drug binding is monovalent to each cell pool, at equilibrium, and fixed at time zero. Bridges are assumed rare relative to singly bound drug in the mass balance. Surface cross-linking constants are taken as proportional to the solution affinities, with no cooperativity, avidity, receptor internalization, shedding, or soluble antigen. The drug changes what a contact does, not how often contacts happen. Relative EC50 is measured against each well’s own plateau.
Inference. Rate constants are declared, not fitted. There is no confidence coverage, no power calculation, and no cost weighting. The indices describe time zero. A lysis endpoint alone cannot identify the encounter coefficient and the per-contact kill probability separately; that is the point of the contact read.
Verification. The code is tested against analytic limits, conservation laws, and scaling identities. Software verification is not biological validation. Presets are synthetic and do not describe any specific molecule, cell product, or assay.
